CRO Testing Roadmap

August 2026

Hale has strong clinical proof and clear customer trust, but slow mobile pages and inconsistent messages interrupt the path to purchase. This roadmap first clears those barriers, then brings the strongest proof and product discovery into the moments where shoppers decide.

The testing sequence

Five focused opportunities

Each concept shows the customer-facing change.

Slot 1Immediate Fix

Homepage and PDP Page Speed

Pages: Homepage and Hale Heart PDP

Mobile visitors wait 7.5 seconds for the homepage’s largest element and 5.9 seconds on the PDP, while both pages take about 20 seconds to become interactive. That delay affects the entire paid funnel before a shopper can evaluate Hale’s claims. The proposed experience keeps the same branded pages but makes their key content visible and usable much sooner.

* Key content becomes visible and interactive sooner while the Hale experience stays familiar. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 2A/B Test

Homepage Hero CTA and Trust

Page: Homepage

The homepage hero shows the lifestyle image and headline, but no purchase path or review signal appears in the first fold. Shoppers must scroll before they can act, even though the PDP already carries a 4.8 rating and 1,342 reviews. The variation places that existing proof and the familiar Get Started button directly in the hero.

* The hero adds the established rating and a direct Get Started action. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 3A/B Test

Cart Cross-Sell

Page: Cart drawer, site-wide

The cart drawer shows Hale Heart, a reservation timer, a discount field, and checkout actions, but it does not introduce Hale’s other product lines. That leaves no simple way to add a complementary product before checkout. The variation adds one compact, one-click Hale Preserve or Hale Longevity recommendation below the cart item.

* A compact one-click recommendation appears between the line item and checkout. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 4Immediate Fix

Homepage and PDP Pricing

Pages: Homepage and Hale Heart PDP

The collected homepage and PDP show different prices for the same 30, 90, and 150-day tiers. This may be a pricing update between collection dates, but it must be confirmed before price-related testing. The corrected journey presents one verified price set and one consistent multi-buy story from homepage to product page.

* The homepage and PDP use one confirmed tier structure and consistent multi-buy language. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 5Immediate Fix

FU Dose Claim

Page: Google Ads creative

One captured paid-search graphic says “10,000 FU Dose,” while the collected homepage, PDP, and other search creative say “10,800 FU.” The conflict affects the clinical-dose claim that introduces Hale to paid visitors. The corrected creative uses the verified figure consistently before the shopper reaches the site.

* The paid creative uses one verified dose figure that matches the on-site claim. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Cross-source themes

Biggest Killers of Conversion Rate

  • Mobile page speed is a funnel-wide constraint. Homepage LCP is 7.5 seconds, PDP LCP is 5.9 seconds, and Time to Interactive is near 20 seconds on both pages.
  • Strong credibility arrives late. The PDP shows a 4.8 rating, 1,342 reviews, and trust modules, while the collected homepage folds show no rating or review count.
  • Pricing and offer messages do not align cleanly across the collected homepage, PDP, and paid-search creative. The homepage and PDP show different tier prices, while discount badges use a separate percentage-off framing.

What the data showed

Google Ads

  • Clinical claims lead the captured creative, including 10,800 FU nattokinase, 36% arterial plaque breakdown, blood-pressure support, and a seven-ingredient formula.
  • One graphic says “10,000 FU Dose,” conflicting with the 10,800 FU figure used elsewhere in the collected creative and site screenshots.
  • Discount badges of “-33%” and “-60%” do not have an obvious equivalent in the collected on-site multi-buy framing.

Page Speed Insights

  • Mobile homepage performance scored 68/100, with 7.5-second LCP and 19.7-second Time to Interactive.
  • Mobile PDP performance scored 69/100, with 5.9-second LCP and 20.9-second Time to Interactive.
  • CLS was not a problem in the collected tests: 0.028 on the homepage and 0 on the PDP.

Site Screenshots

  • The homepage hero has no CTA. The first Get Started action appears in fold two.
  • No rating or review count appears in the three collected homepage folds, while the PDP displays 4.8 stars and 1,342 reviews above the product title.
  • The cart contains one Hale Heart line item, a reservation timer, a discount field, and checkout actions, but no cross-sell, bundle, or free-shipping progress bar.
  • The PDP uses three multi-buy tiers, with Buy 2 Get 1 FREE preselected and visually emphasized.

Reviews and UGC

  • Specific positive testimonials mention blood pressure changing from 152/92 to 121/78 and a follow-up scan showing no progression.
  • Circulation, replacing several supplement bottles, improved energy, and reduced fatigue recur in the fetched review themes.
  • No complaint-themed reviews surfaced. Because no raw review export was collected, this remains a data gap rather than evidence of no customer friction.

Live Site Review

  • The live homepage showed $44.99, $89.99, and $134.99 for the 30, 90, and 150-day tiers.
  • The collected PDP screenshot showed $39.99 per month, $79.99, and $119.99 against a $44.99 per month reference. The difference may reflect a pricing update between collection dates and requires confirmation.

Competitor Analysis

  • Hale’s doctor-formulated, peer-reviewed, cGMP, clinical-dose positioning differentiates it from commodity nattokinase options.
  • Toku Flow uses a structured health journey with baseline tracking, a 90-day retest, and a completion reward, directly addressing the gradual-results pattern found in Hale reviews.
