# Non-Data Context (brand-provided strategy notes)

## Where we stand
August ran on a giveaway (record month at 200k), and September shows what is left once that falls away. The site converts normally at 2.5%, the same as before the giveaway, and AOV is slightly higher. The problem sits entirely on the buying side: the same creatives are burnt out, CTR dropped 36% and the cost per click rose 43%. We are buying less traffic, and paying more for it.

## Metrics: revenue per link click

| Per link click | 1-4 Aug | 25-31 Aug | 1-4 Sept |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost | € 1.38 | € 1.39 | € 1.75 (+27%) |
| Revenue (blended) | € 14.41 | € 14.49 | € 10.56 (-27%) |
| Revenue (Meta attributed) | € 8.29 | € 8.89 | € 5.93 |

We pay 27% more per click and get 27% less out of it. That is the double hit. Simply pushing more budget in does not fix this, it only makes the same leaking funnel more expensive.

## The strategy in one line
Bring in more traffic at the top of the funnel at a lower click price, and get more revenue out of every click. We do that with a fixed offer and landing pages that match the angle someone came in on. Not one or the other: both at the same time, because they reinforce each other. Since the B2B is declining in rev, we don't need to pay attention to them and an evergreen order could be the fix to the inconsistent performance.

## Four priorities

### 1. Evergreen offer: revenue per click up
Right now the offer is essentially "discount", and you can see it in the numbers: discount pressure went from 12% to 20.5% of gross revenue while volume halved. We are training people to wait for the next promotion. A fixed, always available offer (a bundle, a volume benefit, or a gift above a spend threshold) replaces that. It lifts order value and conversion without structurally giving away margin, and it does not depend on a campaign moment. This is the fastest lever on that € 10.56. At current volume, one extra euro per click shows up immediately.

### 2. Landing pages per angle and persona
All traffic currently lands on roughly the same page, regardless of the story someone came in on. That is where the biggest untapped opportunity sits: the click is already paid for, but conversion after the click has barely been optimised. We want a dedicated page per main angle and persona, where the promise from the ad comes back word for word in the first scroll. This is the second half of the revenue per click equation and deserves priority in the coming period.

### 3. Content genuinely moving to top of funnel
The current set mainly speaks to people who already know the brand. That is why frequency is climbing (6.3 on the bid cap campaign across four days) on shrinking reach, while CTR drops. We need new content aimed at cold audiences: problem recognition, education, social proof, more volume and more variation in hooks. Mango Media will step in here to provide the production capacity. The goal is a continuous supply rather than batches, so fatigue no longer builds up to this level.

### 4. Tracking
On whether we should add a third party tracking tool such as Elevar or WeTracked, our recommendation is not at this stage.

We reviewed the current setup and it is in good shape. Most importantly, the Conversions API is already live. That is the server side connection which sends purchase data directly from the store to Meta, rather than relying on the visitor's browser. Over the past year it has sent roughly as many events as the browser tracking does, around 94,000 each, which tells us it is genuinely doing its job: catching the orders the browser misses because of ad blockers, privacy settings and cookie restrictions.

The data quality coming through it is strong as well. Meta scores the purchase event at 9.3 out of 10 on match quality, with email, phone, name, city, postcode and country all matched at 100%.
