CRO testing roadmap

September 2026

Hears has strong product appeal, but key conversion surfaces tell different stories about use case, trust, availability, and value. This roadmap aligns the message shoppers click with the experience they see, removes interaction delays, and makes purchase decisions easier.

Testing ideas

Slot 1A/B Test

Homepage Message Match

Page: Homepage

Offer-focused traffic arrives with sleep, moto, festival, or concert intent, while the homepage opens on one Pacha festival story. The first screen can feel unrelated to what the shopper clicked. The variation keeps the Hears layout but matches its headline, supporting image, and CTA to the promised use case.

The first screen changes from a universal Pacha message to the shopper's promised use case. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 2Immediate Fix

Homepage and PDP Interactivity

Pages: Homepage and product detail page

The audit recorded fast visual paint but 8,590 ms of blocking time on the homepage and 17,600 ms on the PDP. The pages can look ready while buttons, swatches, and carousels remain delayed. The fix preserves the storefront experience while letting core shopping controls respond before non-essential widgets load.

Core shopping controls become responsive before chat, video, and review modules load. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 3A/B Test

On-Site Rating Clarity

Pages: Homepage and product detail page

The homepage still says 4.9/5 from 200,000+ customers, while the live PDP now shows 4.8 from 4,728 reviews and independent platforms report lower scores. Shoppers who compare sources see different trust stories. The variation ties the visible on-site rating to its current review count and names it clearly as on-site proof.

The rating becomes specific about its source and current on-site review count. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 4Validation-First A/B Test

Color Availability Status

Page: Hears product detail page

The audit capture showed a struck-through color beside a general in-stock message, but Pacha Edition 2.0 is currently available. The unavailable-swatch interaction needs a fresh check before this test is scoped. If the contradiction recurs, the selected color should show its exact status and point shoppers to available alternatives.

When an unavailable color is selected, the stock line names that exact state and offers a clear next step. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 5A/B Test

Cart Threshold Recommendations

Page: Cart drawer

The captured cart was $38 from free shipping but showed a $43 core product as its visible recommendation. That option overshoots the gap and hides the $9 to $12 accessory tier. The variation leads with a lower-cost accessory while keeping the core-product recommendation available.

The cart leads with $9 and $10 accessories while keeping the $43 core product available. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 6Validation-First A/B Test

Traffic-to-Variant Alignment

Page: Hears product detail page

The audit found that search links could carry shoppers into a Pacha-specific PDP state while the clean product URL resolved to Brass Blue. The live storefront can now resolve a different default by context, so each entry path needs fresh validation. The variation preserves the intended, in-stock color from the click through the PDP instead of relying on a stale default.

The in-stock color selected before the click remains selected on the PDP. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 7A/B Test

PDP Guarantee Placement

Page: Hears product detail page

The 100-day money-back guarantee sits below Add to Cart, while the 2 Year Warranty and 100-Day Free Returns appear in the cart drawer. Shoppers reach the purchase decision before seeing the complete reassurance set. The variation places all three promises together above the rating and price without changing their wording.

The full guarantee and warranty set moves above the rating, price, and Add to Cart decision. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

If reproduced, shoppers are browsing product names without the photography that makes the ranges distinct. What the data says

Google Ads

  • Paid-search messaging separates sleep, motorbike, festival, concert, and music use cases, but the verified homepage opens on the Pacha festival story for all traffic.
  • Paid-search offers include Festival Season - 45% Off and Buy 2 & Get 10% Off, while the live sitewide offer says Buy 2, Get 1 Free.
  • Three third-party resellers appear in Shopping listings alongside Hears' verified account using Hears product imagery.

PageSpeed Insights

  • The homepage recorded 1.7s LCP and 0.001 CLS, but 8,590 ms Total Blocking Time and 65.6s Time to Interactive.
  • The PDP recorded 2.4s LCP and 0.001 CLS, but 17,600 ms Total Blocking Time and 85.9s Time to Interactive.
  • Both pages paint quickly but become interactive much later.

Site Screenshots

  • The homepage repeats the Pacha seasonal story above the first visible paths to Sleep and Motosports use cases.
  • The captured cart was $38 from free shipping and displayed a $43 core product as the visible cross-sell.
  • The collection captures showed gray image blocks in the lower Hears Sleep, Accessories, and Merch grids. The audit could not confirm whether this was persistent or caused by lazy-load timing.

Social and Community Research

  • Trustpilot shows a 3.5 TrustScore from 1,284 reviews, and Amazon shows 3.8/5 from 232 ratings for the Hears One Ear Plugs listing.
  • The Saint Laurent x Hears collaboration places the brand in a fashion-adjacent lane not observed in the collected homepage or PDP messaging.
  • Hears' TikTok account had 26.8K likes and 889 followers in the collected research.

Reviews and UGC

  • Comfort for side and stomach sleepers, snoring reduction, and concert use without post-event ringing recur across reviews.
  • Customers call out the XS, S, and M tip sizes as useful for small or unusual ear canals.
  • The collected on-site set was overwhelmingly five-star, with two four-star reviews and none at three stars or below.
  • Cleaning kits and carry pouches receive repeated positive mentions as accessory add-ons.

Competitor Research

  • Hears sits around $43 to $46 per set, above Loop's sleep line and Mack's, and near Eargasm.
  • Hears claims both sleep and concert or high-fidelity use cases in one SKU, broader than the single-product claims observed for Loop, Eargasm, and Mack's.
  • Hears reviews name Flares, Zound, and Alpine as products shoppers compared before purchasing.
