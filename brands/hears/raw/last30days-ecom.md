## Findings by Source

### Trustpilot

- Hears (hears.com) carries a TrustScore of 3.5 based on 1,284 reviews. Considering the most recent ~1,042 reviews, most reviewers report being "somewhat happy" overall — not uniformly positive.
- Recurring positive themes: comfort, lightweight fit, effectiveness at reducing everyday background noise (snoring, traffic, concerts), tidy/premium packaging, reusable design seen as cleaner than disposable foam alternatives.
- Note: a separate related entity, "Hears Hearing & Hearables" (hearshearingandhearables.com), has its own distinct Trustpilot profile — do not conflate the two brands when citing review volume.

### Amazon

- "Hears One Ear Plugs" (Jet Black, 20dB SNR) listing: 3.8/5 from 232 ratings — notably lower than the 4.9/5 shown on the brand's own PDP screenshot, and lower than the 4.9/5 "Rated by 200,000+ customers" claim on the homepage. Worth flagging as a cross-platform rating discrepancy for audit verification.
- Hears does not appear to dominate its Amazon category — competitor products (Yawsoy, Mack's, DEWALT, Eargasm, HEAROS, EarPeace, and others) appear alongside it in the same search results, several with higher review counts.

### TikTok

- Confirmed active brand presence (@hears.com), plus organic creator content tagging the brand.
- One creator video (@valerywho, ~5K views): "probando mis primeros earplugs porque me rehúso a quedarme sorda con el tiempo 👂❌ @hears" — first-earplug-purchase framing tied to hearing-loss anxiety, Spanish-language audience.
- General hearing-protection awareness content in this space (not always brand-tagged) performs well engagement-wise, suggesting category-level educational content is a viable content angle even without direct Hears mentions.

### Pinterest

- Strongest and most distinctive signal in this run: a Saint Laurent (YSL) x Hears collaboration — pins reference "Luxury meets festival" positioning, a customer pairing "YSL x Hears Earplugs" with a fashion accessory, and web results independently confirm a "Sound Check, Fashion Check: Saint Laurent x Hears Earplugs Drop" feature (fuckingyoung.es). This positions Hears in a fashion/luxury-adjacent lane distinct from purely functional hearing-protection competitors (Loop, Eargasm, Mack's).
- A design agency's pins reference producing "exploded view" 3D product animations for @hearsearplugs, suggesting recent investment in product-visualization content.
- Most other Pinterest results returned were competitor products (Loop, Eargasm, Hearprotek, LYSIAN, Bollsen) surfaced by the same search terms, not Hears-specific.

### Web

- Official hears.com product page for "Protective Bumper Black" surfaced directly: 4.9/5 from 73 reviews, $12 sale price, "In stock ready to ship" — consistent with the cart-drawer and PDP cross-sell screenshots collected separately.
- No independent editorial/listicle coverage of Hears specifically was found in this pass (a competing "7 best earplugs for concerts" listicle from The Independent did not include Hears).

### YouTube

- A direct branded review video exists: "Hears One & Hears Plus Earplug Review: My Tinnitus Changed How I Protect My Hearing" (identified at precheck, not pulled into ranked evidence this pass — worth a manual follow-up look during audit).
- Beyond that, no other Hears-specific YouTube content scored meaningfully in this run; most YouTube results returned were unrelated (other brands' earbud reviews, unrelated reaction content) and were filtered out as noise.

## Cross-Source Signal

The YSL x Hears luxury/fashion collaboration is corroborated independently across Pinterest and Web (fuckingyoung.es editorial), suggesting a real fashion-crossover narrative worth surfacing in the audit. Separately, a rating discrepancy shows up between the brand's own site (4.9/5, 200,000+ customers) and third-party platforms — Amazon shows 3.8/5 (232 ratings) and Trustpilot shows a 3.5 TrustScore (1,284 reviews) — a gap large enough to flag for verification rather than treat as noise.
