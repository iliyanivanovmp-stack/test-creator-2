CRO Testing Roadmap

August 2026

Paid traffic arrives with clear training, health, and sampling intent, but the first purchase areas often lead with configuration instead. This roadmap carries those promises into the decision point, strengthens premium value, and makes trust easier to see.

Eight focused experiments

The testing roadmap

Slot 1A/B Test

Taste Tester Symptom Match

Page: Taste Tester PDP

Health-focused traffic arrives with a gut, itching, and joint promise, while the first purchase area leads with the sample mechanics. The reason for the click is separated from the buying decision. The variation adds a compact digestion, skin and coat, and joint-support block above the offer.

The health promise moves into the first purchase area. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 2A/B Test

Starter Pack Use Cases

Page: Starter Pack PDP

Trainer-led traffic reaches a buy box that asks shoppers to choose recipe and autoship before it explains the relevant use case. Reviews already describe Eureka as a treat, topper, and enrichment food. The variation adds those three uses directly under the review row.

Training rewards, toppers, and enrichment become visible before product configuration. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 3A/B Test

Sample-First Homepage Hero

Page: Homepage

The homepage hero sends every visitor to a generic shop path, while the Taste Tester offers a smaller first step for fussy-dog shoppers. The variation keeps the split layout and dog imagery, but leads with the $19.99 Taste Tester and moves the general shop path to a text link.

The Taste Tester becomes the primary homepage path, with the general shop link retained. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 4A/B Test

Premium Value Block

Pages: Starter Pack and Taste Tester PDPs

Price is the clearest objection in reviews, even when customers still want the product as a treat or topper. Positive reviews justify the cost through no fillers, no crumbs, smaller feeds, and dogs eating it. The variation adds a compact value block for full meals, toppers, training, and enrichment near price.

The premium price is paired with concrete ways to use every bag. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 5A/B Test

Autoship Consent and Control

Page: Starter Pack PDP

Autoship is selected with perks, but renewal, cancellation, and shopper control are under-explained at the decision point. A negative review says the shopper never signed up for more, while positive reviews praise pause and delay controls. The variation makes recurring delivery and account control explicit inside the selected panel.

Renewal and shopper controls become part of the selected autoship choice. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 6A/B Test

Taste Tester Health Proof

Page: Taste Tester PDP

Offer-focused traffic carries an 86% health-improvement claim, but the first purchase area does not show that proof near the button. Shoppers see the sample offer without the strongest reason to believe behind it. The variation pairs the 86% claim with the 4.9 rating directly above Add to cart.

The health-improvement claim and review proof sit directly above the purchase button. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 7A/B Test

Homepage Proof Upgrade

Page: Homepage

The hero proof shows Trustpilot and 76,000+ dogs, but omits the review themes that make Eureka distinctive. Reviews repeatedly mention fussy dogs eating it, shinier coats, reduced stomach trouble, no fillers, and flexible subscriptions. The variation adds a compact proof row beneath the existing trust line.

Review-backed reasons to believe join the existing Trustpilot proof. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 8A/B Test

First-Fold Performance

Pages: Homepage and Taste Tester PDP

The homepage recorded 11.8s LCP and 0.641 CLS, while the Taste Tester recorded 20.3s Speed Index and 1,223 KiB of estimated unused JavaScript. Slow completion and layout movement can delay the content shoppers need first. The test keeps the visible experience intact while stabilizing first-fold media and prioritizing the hero, buy box, quantity controls, and CTA.

Critical first-fold elements load into reserved space without changing the visible design. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Cross-source themes

Biggest Killers of Conversion Rate

  • Message continuationTraining and health promises from paid traffic are not consistently repeated in the corresponding PDP first folds.
  • Premium-price anxietyReviews repeatedly call Eureka expensive, while positive reviews justify the cost through no waste, smaller feeds, and flexible use.
  • Trust at risk pointsStrong ratings and Australian-made proof sit alongside concerns about subscription misunderstanding, delivery, support access, login, and gift fit.

What the evidence says

Source findings

Meta Ads and Landing Pages

  • Trainer-led traffic reaches the Starter Pack PDP, where recipe and subscription choices appear before training use cases.
  • The $19.99 sample offer matches the Taste Tester PDP, but the 86% health-improvement proof is not visible in the collected first three folds.
  • Gut, itching, and joint language appears in paid creative, while related benefit copy sits below the early purchase area.

Google Ads

  • Search messaging covers feeding calculators, Australian ownership, all-natural food, high-protein treats, air-dried food, and sample offers.
  • Offers include 17% off Starter Pack, 24% discount badges, Buy 4 Get 15% Off, and Try a sample today.
  • Search messaging emphasizes category, origin, and range, while paid-social messaging emphasizes health improvement, trial, and the $19.99 sample.

Reviews and UGC

  • Customers repeatedly mention fussy dogs eating Eureka and improvements in coat, digestion, energy, weight, and vitality.
  • Price is the clearest barrier. Review language includes “beyond my budget,” “still very expensive,” and “ridiculously expensive.”
  • Treat, topper, training, scatter-mat, Kong, and enrichment use gives budget-sensitive customers more ways to adopt the product.
  • Subscription flexibility earns praise, while one reviewer said they “never signed up for more.”

Page Speed Insights

  • Homepage performance scored 25/100, with 11.8s LCP, 0.641 CLS, 560ms TBT, and 1,229 KiB of estimated unused JavaScript.
  • Taste Tester performance scored 54/100, with 4.6s LCP, 20.3s Speed Index, 550ms TBT, and 1,223 KiB of estimated unused JavaScript.
  • Lighthouse linked homepage layout shift to main content, the hero bottom group, and media elements without explicit sizes.

Site Screenshots and Live Pages

  • The homepage hero uses “Real Food Real Easy,” an orange Shop Now CTA, Trustpilot proof, and 76,000+ happy dogs, but no Taste Tester path.
  • The Starter Pack buy box includes 4.9 from 303 reviews, recipe choice, autoship at $169.15, one-time purchase at $199.00, and a black Add to cart CTA.
  • The Taste Tester buy box includes 4.9 from 3,707 reviews, seven 20g samples, free delivery, $20 subscription credit, and a $19.99 CTA path.

Competitor Research

  • Frontier Pets shows daily-cost framing, including an approximate $4.50 per day example for a 10kg dog.
  • Lyka leads with personalized portions, clinical gut and stool proof, and a delivery subscription.
  • Eureka can distinguish itself through Australian sourcing, sample-first trial, shelf-stable convenience, and subscription perks.

Brand Context

  • The requested test scope stays on the homepage, Starter Pack landing page, and shared Taste Tester landing page.
  • Collection-page screenshots are reference context only and are excluded from this roadmap.
