# Eureka CRO Research Brief

**Data Sources:** Manifest, Meta summaries, live PDP text, Google Ads, Trustpilot 2026-06-15, PageSpeed 2026-08-18, context, site summary, live homepage, competitor research 2026-08-18.

Paid traffic arrives with clear intent, but the first purchase areas do not always continue that intent. Meta Ad 1 asks, "What food do you use in your training?" The Starter Pack PDP then asks shoppers to choose recipe, frequency, autoship, and quantity before it explains trainer, topper, or treat-pouch use. Source: Meta summaries, live PDP text, site summary, Trustpilot 2026-06-15.

The Taste Tester page has the same gap for health-problem traffic. Ad 3 promises relief from gut, itching, and joint problems. The first fold sells a $19.99 sample with seven 20g samples, free delivery, $20 credit, quantity controls, and a black CTA, but it does not repeat gut, itching, or joint language near the CTA. Source: Meta summaries, live PDP text, site summary.

Reviews validate both paths. Customers mention treat pouches, Kongs, scatter mats, toppers, and rewards. They also cite shinier coats, better sleep, more energy, weight control, reduced stomach trouble, healthier dogs, and happier dogs. Source: Trustpilot 2026-06-15.

Price and trust are the main objections. Reviewers call Eureka "beyond my budget," "still very expensive," and "ridiculously expensive." Positive reviews justify the price through no fillers, no crumbs, smaller feeds, flexible subscriptions, and dogs eating it. Source: Trustpilot 2026-06-15.

Performance also slows the first impression. Homepage Lighthouse performance was 25/100 on 2026-08-18, with LCP 11.8s and CLS 0.641. Taste Tester PDP performance was 54/100, with LCP 4.6s, Speed Index 20.3s, and unused JavaScript savings estimated at 1,223 KiB. Source: PageSpeed 2026-08-18.

## Slot 1: Symptom-Match Taste Tester Hero

**Type:** A/B test (1 variation vs. control)
**Page:** Taste Tester PDP (https://eurekapet.co/products/natural-dog-food-sample)
**Revenue potential:** unknown sessions/mo x 3% CR lift x unknown AOV = unknown monthly revenue.

**Hypothesis:** If we continue the gut, itching, and joint promise from Meta Ad 3 in the first purchase area, more paid visitors will add the Taste Tester because the page will answer the problem they clicked for before asking them to buy.

**Data:** Meta Ad 3 says "Say Bye to Gut, Itching, Joint Problems." The Taste Tester fold shows 4.9/3707 reviews, $19.99, seven 20g samples, free delivery, $20 credit, quantity controls, and black CTA, but does not repeat gut, itching, or joint language near the CTA. Source: Meta summaries, live PDP text, site summary.

**V1:** On mobile, place a compact benefit block between the review rating and the $19.99 offer that says the Taste Tester helps shoppers find the recipe fit for digestion, skin and coat, and joint support. On desktop, place the same block in the buy box above the sample copy. Keep the gallery, price, quantity controls, free delivery, $20 credit, and black CTA unchanged.

## Future Slot Candidates

1. **Cart Drawer Risk-Reversal And Credit Reminder** - The cart drawer has checkout and cross-sell mechanics, but no visible guarantee, delivery reassurance, subscription clarity, or $20 credit reminder at checkout.
2. **Small-Dog Gift Fit Reassurance** - Autoship perks promise welcome gifts and free treats for life, but reviews cite goat horn and croissant gift mismatch for small dogs.
3. **Google Search Landing Page Variants** - Google Ads include feeding calculator, Australian-owned, all-natural food, high-protein treats, and sample messaging, which could support search-specific landing page tests.
4. **Contact And Subscription Help Visibility** - Reviews mention no phone number, hard-to-find contact paths, and login code problems, which could support a future trust and support visibility test.
