CRO Testing Roadmap

August 2026

Revival has strong trust assets across the funnel, but the buy action, price anchor, and message match often arrive one step late. This roadmap keeps the purchase path visible, brings existing proof closer to the decision, and makes paid traffic land on the promise it clicked.

Testing Sequence

Eight customer-facing tests

Each card shows the page surface, the conversion tension, and the visible customer experience to test.

Slot 1A/B Test

Sticky PDP Add to Cart

Page: Product Detail Page

The PDP Add to Cart button is static in fold 1, while trust icons, tabs, subscription perks, and UGC continue below it. Shoppers lose the purchase action while reading the content meant to convince them. The variation keeps a compact product bar with price and Add to Cart visible after the buy box scrolls away.

* The purchase action stays visible while shoppers read proof and education content. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 2A/B Test

PDP Compare-At Pricing

Page: Product Detail Page

The PDP buy box shows per-stick pricing, but the cart shows £39.99 struck through against £31.99 for the same product. The stronger discount anchor appears after the shopper has already made the add-to-cart decision. The variation brings the struck-through price into the buy box while keeping per-stick pricing as supporting text.

* The cart's struck-through price appears inside the PDP purchase option. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 3A/B Test

Travel Landing Page Hero

Page: Travel paid-social landing page

Offer-focused travel traffic clicks a hot-climate hydration promise, then lands on the generic "Your Best Day, Every Day" hero. That breaks the story at the first screen. The variation carries the travel hook into the landing page with getaway imagery, hot-climate hydration copy, and the same clear Shop Now action.

* The first screen repeats the travel promise instead of defaulting to generic brand messaging. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 4A/B Test

Neutral Purchase Options

Page: Product Detail Page

The buy box preselects Subscribe & Save 20% with a blue border, while One-time purchase sits below as a plain option. That makes it hard to separate genuine subscription preference from the default visual nudge. The variation gives both choices equal weight and asks shoppers to choose before Add to Cart activates, while preserving the subscription benefit stack.

* Subscribe and one-time purchase start as equal choices before the shopper selects one. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 5A/B Test

Advertorial CTA Placement

Page: Late-night advertorial landing page

The advertorial opens with a headline and byline, then asks readers to scroll into the article before the primary "Get 15% Off Revival Shots" button appears. Readers who do not reach the middle of the article only see a small header link as the buying path. The variation adds the same CTA directly under the headline and byline, while keeping the existing mid-article CTA in place.

* The same discount CTA appears immediately under the advertorial headline. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 6A/B Test

Collection SKU Ratings

Page: Collection page

The collection page uses category cards, with ratings and review counts attached to the range rather than a specific product or flavor. Some cards show very low category-level counts beside the brand's broader review claims. The variation swaps that line for the featured SKU's rating and count so shoppers judge a concrete product before clicking.

* Category-level social proof changes to the featured product's own rating line. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 7A/B Test

Homepage Fold 1 Trust

Page: Homepage

The athlete quote and press logos sit in fold 2, after the static Shop Now hero. Competitor research shows those trust assets are more distinctive than the category-standard electrolyte claim. The variation adds a compact press and athlete trust strip below the hero CTA while leaving the full trust section lower on the page.

* Press and athlete proof move into the first fold beside the hero CTA. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 8A/B Test

Homepage Discount Cleanup

Page: Homepage

The header trust bar states "15% Off For New Customers," and a separate floating "Get 15% Off" pill repeats the same offer across the homepage. The same discount competes with itself in two places. The variation removes the floating pill and keeps the offer once in the header trust bar.

* The duplicate floating discount pill is removed while the header offer remains. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Biggest Killers of Conversion Rate

Where the funnel loses clarity

  • Primary CTAs are static across the homepage, PDP, and paid-social landing pages, so the buy path disappears while shoppers scroll through proof, testimonials, and education.
  • The travel promise has no landing page support and no Google Ads counterpart. A shopper clicks hot-climate hydration and arrives at generic brand messaging.
  • The PDP hides the strongest discount framing until cart. The buy box shows unit pricing, while the cart shows £39.99 struck through against £31.99.
  • Distinctive trust assets sit below the first homepage hero, even though athlete and press proof are less matched by competitors than the electrolyte claim.

Source Findings

Meta Ads and Landing Pages

  • The late-night recovery path keeps its promise consistent from creative to landing page.
  • The travel path breaks on landing: hot-climate hydration and getaway packing lead to the generic "Your Best Day, Every Day" template.
  • The advertorial path uses a distinct listicle format, with "Get 15% Off Revival Shots" appearing mid-article rather than near the opening headline.
  • Primary CTAs are static across the captured landing templates.

Google Ads Transparency

  • Google Ads lean on category-authority claims such as "UK's No.1 Rehydration Powder" and "Most Reviewed."
  • The hangover and late-night angle is consistent across paid channels.
  • The travel angle has no Google Ads counterpart in the collected data.

Current Site Screenshots

  • The homepage repeats the same 15% offer in the header trust bar and a floating bottom-left pill.
  • Collection cards show range-level ratings and review counts rather than SKU-level proof.
  • The PDP defaults to Subscribe & Save 20% with a blue border, while One-time purchase is plain and unselected.
  • The cart already shows £39.99 struck through against £31.99 for the 30 Pack Assorted Flavours product.

Live Homepage Fetch

  • The live homepage provides the same blue product-led hero language used in the screenshot set.
  • Brand assets include a wide product banner, mobile hero banner, product pack photography, and the logo asset.

Competitor Research

  • SOS Rehydrate and Liquid I.V. both use electrolyte-performance claims that overlap with Revival's core hydration positioning.
  • Revival's press logo strip and athlete endorsement are less matched by SOS Rehydrate in the collected competitor review.
  • The competitor scan flags the subscription-first buy box and unsupported travel angle as Revival-specific weaknesses.
