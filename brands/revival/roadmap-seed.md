# Revival Roadmap Seed

**Store:** https://revivalshots.com/
**AOV:** ~£30-40 (based on product pricing: 12-pack £19.99, 30-pack £39.99, subscription price £31.99)
**Monthly sessions:** unknown — not collected
**Data sources:** Meta Ads and Landing Pages, Google Ads Transparency, Current Site Screenshots, live homepage fetch, competitor research

## Key Insights

Revival's paid media and site share one structural gap: the primary CTA is static, non-sticky, on every page type screenshotted — homepage, PDP, and all three Meta landing page templates. Trust content (press logos, UGC carousels, athlete endorsement, FAQs) is dense and stacks below the fold, meaning the "Shop Now" or "Add to Cart" action disappears from view exactly while users are consuming the content meant to convince them to buy.

Message match breaks down specifically on the travel angle. Meta Ad 2 ("2026 Travel Hack — stay hydrated in the hottest climates") sends traffic to the generic "Your Best Day, Every Day" homepage template with no travel imagery, copy, or offer, and Google Ads has no travel-angle counterpart at all — it's an isolated creative running with zero on-site or cross-channel support. By contrast, Ads 1 and 3 (hangover/late-night recovery) match tightly with both landing pages and Google's search ad themes.

Pricing transparency is inconsistent across the funnel: the PDP buy box shows only per-stick unit pricing (£1.07 vs £1.33) with no compare-at discount shown, while the cart displays a clear struck-through £39.99 → £31.99 for the identical product. The stronger price anchor exists only in the cart template, not at the point of the add-to-cart decision. Separately, the PDP defaults hard into "Subscribe & Save 20%" (blue border, visually emphasized) over one-time purchase (plain border, unselected) — an aggressive, deliberate nudge worth isolating with a neutral-hierarchy test. Competitor research (SOS Rehydrate, Liquid I.V.) shows Revival's "3x electrolytes" claim is category-standard, not a differentiator — the athlete endorsement and press-as-seen-in logos are Revival's stronger, less-matched trust assets and currently sit in fold 2, after the hero.

## Top Test Opportunities

### 1. Sticky Add-to-Cart Bar on PDP
**What's broken:** On the product detail page, the buy box sits in fold 1: product image with nav arrows on the left, title/price/rating/value-prop bullets on the right, then the "Subscribe & Save 20%" vs. "One-time purchase" radio options, box-quantity selector, and a full-width dark "ADD TO CART" button. This button is static — once a user scrolls into fold 2 (trust icon row, video module, tabbed Description/Benefits/Research content) or fold 3 (Subscribe & Save promo, UGC carousel), there is no persistent bar or floating CTA bringing "Add to Cart" back into view. The purchase action is only reachable by scrolling back up.
**Evidence:** site-visual-summary.md (PDP fold 1-3, CTA behavior)
**Key data:** PDP spans 3 folds of trust/education content (feature icon row, tabbed content, subscribe perks, testimonials) all below a non-sticky CTA.
**Est. lift:** 2-4% CR lift x unknown sessions/mo x £30 AOV — directional, pending traffic data.

### 2. Compare-At Pricing on PDP Buy Box
**What's broken:** The PDP buy box currently shows two purchase options — "Subscribe & Save 20%" at £31.99 and "One-time purchase" at £39.99 — with only per-stick unit pricing displayed (£1.07 vs £1.33 per stick). No strikethrough/compare-at price appears directly in the buy box. Later, in the cart drawer, the same product displays a clear struck-through £39.99 next to a sale price of £31.99. The stronger discount-anchor visual only shows up after the user has already added to cart, not at the moment they're deciding which purchase option to select.
**Evidence:** site-visual-summary.md (PDP buy box detail, Cart line items)
**Key data:** Cart already contains the struck-through pricing asset (£39.99 → £31.99) for the same SKU — no new asset creation needed, only earlier placement.
**Est. lift:** 1-3% CR lift on PDP-to-cart conversion x £30-40 AOV.

### 3. Rebuild Ad 2 Landing Page to Match Travel Hook
**What's broken:** Meta Ad 2's creative and copy are travel-themed: headline "2026 Travel Hack," body copy "Stay hydrated in the hottest climates / Accelerate recovery / Wake up fresh," with a travel/airport-setting thumbnail. Clicking through lands on the same landing page template as Ad 1's generic brand hero: full-width blue banner, product lineup image, headline "Your Best Day, Every Day. Nothing Hydrates Faster," white "Shop Now" button — no travel imagery, no hot-climate copy, no travel-specific offer or bundle. Google Ads Transparency data shows no travel-angle ad exists there either — this creative angle is running in isolation with zero on-site or cross-channel reinforcement.
**Evidence:** meta-ads-visual-summary.md (Ad 2), google-ads-visual-summary.md (gaps vs. Meta)
**Key data:** Ad 2 is one of only 3 active Meta creatives; its landing page is identical in template to Ad 1's, despite a completely different hook.
**Est. lift:** 10-20% relative CVR lift on Ad 2 traffic specifically — directional, ad-level spend/CVR not collected.

### 4. Consolidate Redundant Discount Messaging on Homepage
**What's broken:** The homepage header trust bar (present on every fold) already states "15% Off For New Customers" alongside delivery, review-count, and guarantee badges. Separately, a persistent bottom-left floating pill/tab reading "Get 15% Off" appears across all three homepage folds, layered on top of the page content independent of scroll position. Both elements advertise the identical offer simultaneously in two different visual locations.
**Evidence:** site-visual-summary.md (Homepage, CTA behavior)
**Key data:** Same 15% new-customer offer duplicated in header bar and floating pill across all 3 folds captured.
**Est. lift:** Minor engagement/scroll-depth improvement — not independently revenue-quantifiable from available data.

### 5. Per-SKU Ratings on Collection Page
**What's broken:** The collection page ("Explore Our Ranges") displays a 2-row category grid — Rehydration & Recovery, Zero Sugar Rehydration, Natural Energy & Performance, Super Greens & Vitamins, Kids Multi-Vitamin Squash, Bundle & Save, Taster Packs — where each card shows a single star rating and aggregated review count for the entire category (e.g., "Rehydration & Recovery, from £11.99, 11,648 reviews"), not for an individual product or flavor within it. A shopper cannot compare a specific SKU's social proof before clicking into a category.
**Evidence:** site-visual-summary.md (Collection page, Fold 1, Price display)
**Key data:** 7 category cards, all showing category-level (not SKU-level) review counts; some categories show as low as "1 review" at the category level despite the parent brand claiming 10,000+ total reviews.
**Est. lift:** 1-2% CR lift on collection-to-PDP click-through.

### 6. Neutral-Hierarchy Buy Box Test (Subscribe vs. One-Time)
**What's broken:** The PDP buy box pre-selects "Subscribe & Save 20%" (£31.99) with a blue border and visual emphasis, while "One-time purchase" (£39.99) sits below it in a plain, unselected, unhighlighted border. This is a deliberate, strong visual hierarchy favoring the subscription option by default.
**Evidence:** site-visual-summary.md (PDP, Buy box detail)
**Key data:** Subscribe option includes 20% off first order, 10% off for life, free gifts worth £35.99, free steel water bottle worth £19.99 — a heavily incentivized default with asymmetric visual weight against the alternative.
**Est. lift:** Directional only — diagnostic test to measure true subscribe-vs-one-time preference split, not a guaranteed CR lift.

### 7. Verify/Fix PDP Fold 2 Video Module
**What's broken:** On PDP fold 2, directly below the trust icon row (Easy Mix, 100% Vegan, GMO Free, Great Taste, Fast Acting, Clean & Pure), a video/image module renders mostly blank white space, with only a small partial hand-holding-product visual visible in one corner. This sits directly above the tabbed Description/Benefits/Research/How to Use/Ingredients content module.
**Evidence:** site-visual-summary.md (PDP, Layout anomalies)
**Key data:** Unconfirmed whether this is a live rendering bug (broken module shown to all PDP visitors) or a lazy-load capture artifact — flagged as needing live-site verification before treating as a test candidate.
**Est. lift:** Unquantifiable until confirmed live.

### 8. Earlier CTA Placement in Ad 3 Advertorial
**What's broken:** Ad 3's landing page uses a long-form advertorial template distinct from Ads 1-2 — headline "7 Reasons You Still Feel Off After a Late Night," byline "Hannah Phillips, 4 minute read," followed by numbered listicle sections (dehydration stats, coffee-as-diuretic). The primary CTA, a full-width blue "Get 15% Off Revival Shots" button, is embedded mid-article, after section 2, rather than near the top of the page alongside the header "Shop Now" link.
**Evidence:** meta-ads-visual-summary.md (Ad 3, LP CTA)
**Key data:** CTA button placement is mid-article (after 2 of at least 2+ numbered sections), with only a small header text link as the earlier alternative.
**Est. lift:** 5-10% relative CVR lift on Ad 3 traffic.

## Unused Findings

- Collection page reaches the footer within 2 folds, unusually short for a category-index page — may indicate thin category-level content, but unverified against the live site.
- Cart's "Still Thirsty?" one-click cross-sell module is a strong existing AOV pattern worth replicating on the PDP rather than a problem to fix.
