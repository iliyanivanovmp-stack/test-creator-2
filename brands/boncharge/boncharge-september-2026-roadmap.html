Conversion roadmap

Bon Charge September 2026

Paid traffic is reaching blocked or mismatched purchase paths, while performance and trust inconsistencies make the journey harder to complete. This roadmap fixes the urgent leaks first, then tests clearer promise delivery, faster media, stronger calls to action, and reassurance at checkout.

Six moves to clear the path

Slot 1Immediate Fix

Stock Status on Product Pages

Tortoise Shell Computer Glasses, Crystal Computer Glasses, Red Light Face Mask

All three checked product pages are sold out, and the Red Light Face Mask still shows "In Stock | Limited Quantities Available" alongside "Sold out." The contradiction weakens trust at the exact moment purchase is blocked. Sold-out pages will show one clear status and a back-in-stock path, with no competing availability claim.

The contradictory availability claim becomes one clear sold-out message and a back-in-stock path. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 2Immediate Fix

Currency Across the Journey

Homepage, product pages, and cart

The audit capture showed a dollar free-shipping threshold on the homepage, euro messaging on product pages, and a dollar-priced cart recommendation inside a euro cart. A fresh EUR-session check now renders euro consistently, so the opportunity is to protect that consistency across every locale handoff. Each shopper will see one currency from announcement bar through cart.

The audit-captured currency switches become one consistent EUR journey from entry to checkout. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 3A/B Test

Glasses Landing-Page Promise

Tortoise Shell and Crystal Computer Glasses product pages

Collected paid-social creative promised the full wellness collection, then sent shoppers to a single glasses product page without that framing or the live sitewide discount. The handoff asks the page to explain a different story from the click. The variation names the exact glasses and carries the 25% offer into the first product-page view.

The first product-page view repeats the offer and names the exact glasses shown before the click. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 4A/B Test

Static First View, Deferred Video

Homepage and Red Light Face Mask product page

Audit Lighthouse runs measured 14.7-second homepage LCP and 13.2-second product-page LCP, while the captured pages load autoplaying hero, testimonial, and four-clip video surfaces. The measurement does not isolate video as the cause, so this test checks the relationship directly. Shoppers first see the same branded imagery and copy, while video loads only when it is needed.

The same branded first view renders as a static image, while testimonial video waits until the shopper approaches it. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 5A/B Test

Homepage Sticky CTA

Homepage

The captured homepage relies on one "Shop Best Sellers" button in the hero, while product pages keep an Add to Cart bar visible as shoppers move down the page. Once the hero leaves view, the homepage loses its direct shopping path. The variation carries "Shop Best Sellers" with the shopper after the first fold.

After the hero scrolls away, a persistent bar keeps the existing "Shop Best Sellers" action within reach. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 6A/B Test

Cart Guarantee

Cart drawer

The captured cart drawer shows free shipping, savings, and "Checkout Securely," but no returns or warranty reassurance. The product page already presents "30-Day Easy Returns | 1-Year Warranty" directly under Add to Cart. The variation brings that same promise into the cart immediately above checkout.

The existing returns and warranty promise moves into the cart immediately above checkout. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Meta Ads and Landing Pages

  • The glasses creative promised the full BON CHARGE wellness collection, but the destination was a single product page focused on eye strain.
  • The specific glasses and the 25% sitewide discount live on the landing page were not mentioned in the collected glasses creative.
  • The Red Light Face Mask creative and page carried the closest message match, including the same product and stat-backed skin claims.
  • Live re-verification found every tracked destination sold out, with the mask page showing "Sold out" and "In Stock | Limited Quantities Available" together.

Google Ads

  • Coverage spanned glasses, face-mask, toothbrush, hair-growth, sleep, and general brand terms, a much broader product mix than paid social.
  • Both paid channels used the recurring 25% sitewide offer.
  • Several collected transparency units rendered "500. That's an error" instead of creative. The destination behavior was not diagnosed.

Reviews and UGC

  • All five collected Red Light Face Mask reviews were positive, with praise for routine fit, visible skin results, and responsive support.
  • The sample is too small to identify frustration themes or support product-quality conclusions.
  • One charging-lead issue was self-attributed to user error and resolved by support.

Page Speed Insights

  • The homepage scored 0.16 with 14.7-second LCP, 3,410ms total blocking time, and 44.1-second time to interactive.
  • The Red Light Face Mask page scored 0.32 with 13.2-second LCP, 900ms total blocking time, and 48.0-second time to interactive.
  • Both pages exceeded the 4-second poor-LCP threshold by a wide margin. The data did not isolate the LCP element.

Site Screenshots

  • The homepage combines a large autoplay hero, a second autoplay testimonial, and additional video thumbnails through the third captured fold.
  • The homepage has one non-sticky "Shop Best Sellers" button, while the Red Light Face Mask page keeps an Add to Cart bar visible across all three captured folds.
  • The cart showed free shipping, savings, and checkout copy, but no guarantee, returns, or warranty reassurance.
  • The product page placed "30-Day Easy Returns | 1-Year Warranty" directly below Add to Cart.

Live Site Verification

  • Tortoise Shell Computer Glasses, Crystal Computer Glasses, and the Red Light Face Mask were all sold out during the audit re-check.
  • The mask page still displayed "In Stock | Limited Quantities Available" beside the sold-out state.
  • The audit re-check found the homepage free-shipping threshold in dollars while collected product-page screenshots used euros.

Social and Community Research

  • The automated Trustpilot pull showed a 2.4 TrustScore from 6 reviews for boncharge.com, while the legacy blublox.com profile held 4 stars from more than 1,400 reviews.
  • An independent sauna-blanket review scored the product 7.4/10 and suggested the brand-direct purchase may be preferable to marketplace listings.
  • Paid partnerships on X and affiliate-disclosed YouTube coverage were active, while the Instagram pull was partial and directional only.

Competitor Research

  • Joovv appeared with premium red-light positioning, while Chroma emphasized a multi-device ecosystem.
  • Swanwick and Ra Optics presented more focused blue-light-glasses offers.
  • Search results characterized BON CHARGE by breadth across targeted wearable therapy rather than leadership in one category.
