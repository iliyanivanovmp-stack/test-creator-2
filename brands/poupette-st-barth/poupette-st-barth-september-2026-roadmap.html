Paid traffic arrives with a specific product or craftsmanship promise, then meets generic landing experiences, limited reassurance, and slow product pages. This roadmap connects those promises to the storefront and makes the path to purchase clearer.

Testing Roadmap

Eight focused experiments across landing experiences, product detail pages, collection browsing, and cart.

Slot 1A/B Test

Paid-Traffic Landing Content

Homepage and Search Results

The homepage and Sasha search results do not reflect the product and print stories that shoppers clicked. That disconnect makes a specific promise feel generic at arrival. The variation gives each product story a clear visual, craftsmanship line, and direct route to shop.

The destination carries the product and craftsmanship promise into the first screen. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 2A/B Test

Craftsmanship Near the Buy Box

Product Detail Page

The captured buy box has shipping and payment icons, but no craftsmanship proof near the purchase action. The quality story that appears in acquisition creative is therefore absent at the decision point. The variation adds a concise hand-made and limited-run line above Add to Cart.

A source-backed craftsmanship line appears beside the existing purchase reassurance. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 3A/B Test

PDP Load Performance

Product Detail Page

The audited product page records a 14.3-second Largest Contentful Paint and takes 33.2 seconds to become interactive on mobile. Shoppers wait too long to see and use the purchase experience. The variation presents the same page and content, but makes the primary image and buy box available much sooner.

The product image and buying controls become usable sooner without a visual redesign. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 4A/B Test

Buy Box Sequence

Product Detail Page

Add to Cart currently appears before the color and size controls in the captured page order. The purchase action arrives before the shopper has completed the required choice. The variation places color and size first, followed by the Add to Cart button.

Color and size selection move ahead of the purchase action. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 5A/B Test

Sticky Add to Cart

Product Detail Page

The current Add to Cart button leaves the viewport once shoppers move into description, delivery, returns, and FAQ content. Completing the purchase then requires a return scroll to the buy box. The variation keeps the product, selected size, price, and Add to Cart action visible at the bottom of the screen.

The purchase action follows the shopper through the lower product-page content. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 6A/B Test

Cart Product Discovery

Cart

The captured cart contains the line item, instructions, subtotal, and checkout action, but no related-product or shipping-progress treatment. It gives shoppers no additional product path before checkout. The variation adds a related-product row and a shipping progress area, with the qualifying rule confirmed before launch.

The cart introduces a relevant product and a visible shipping-offer path before checkout. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 7A/B Test

Collection Sale Pricing

New Collection

The captured collection grid mixes original-plus-reduced pricing with single-price product cards. That inconsistency makes sale status harder to recognize while scanning. The variation gives every discounted style the same original-price and sale-price treatment while leaving full-price styles unchanged.

Discounted styles use one clear original-price and sale-price pattern across the grid. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 8A/B Test

Homepage Savoir-Faire

Homepage

Craftsmanship language appears in acquisition creative but not in the first three captured homepage folds. The storefront opens as an editorial lookbook without explaining what makes the pieces distinctive. The variation places a concise Savoir-Faire module between the hero and the Women and Girls panels.

The craftsmanship story enters the homepage before category discovery begins. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Biggest Killers of Conversion Rate

  1. 01

    Message match breaks at arrival

    Product and craftsmanship promises lead into a generic homepage or broad search grid. The destination does not continue the reason the shopper clicked.

  2. 02

    Trust is missing near purchase

    The homepage, PDP buy box, and cart lack review counts, star ratings, or guarantee badges, while craftsmanship claims remain separated from the purchase decision.

  3. 03

    Product pages load too slowly

    The audited PDP records a 14.3-second LCP and 33.2-second Time to Interactive on mobile, adding a severe delay to an already mismatched journey.

Meta Ads and Landing Pages

  • Paid-social creative features a hand-made yellow print, a burgundy print story, and the Sasha as a hero best-seller.
  • The print-led traffic reaches a generic seasonal homepage, while Sasha traffic reaches a broad search grid with no single best-seller foregrounded.
  • The captured landing experiences show no review count, star rating, or guarantee near arrival.

Google Ads

  • Creative emphasizes iconic prints, timeless cuts, handmade production, easy returns, and traditional sewing.
  • A 10% first-order offer is the dominant search message, while a separate 50% Archives promotion runs in parallel.
  • The 50% Archives promotion was not visible in the collected homepage, PDP, or cart screenshots.

PageSpeed Insights

  • The homepage scored 65/100 on mobile, with a 10.3-second LCP and 16.1-second Time to Interactive.
  • The PDP scored 64/100 on mobile, with a 14.3-second LCP and 33.2-second Time to Interactive.
  • Layout stability was not an issue. CLS was 0 on both audited pages.

Site Screenshots

  • The first three homepage folds are dominated by lifestyle photography, with no product grid, pricing, bestseller tiles, or visible trust signals.
  • The PDP places Add to Cart before color and size controls, and the static button leaves the viewport below the buy box.
  • The cart contains one line item, instructions, subtotal, and checkout, with no cross-sell, bundle, shipping-threshold, or guarantee content.
  • The collection grid mixes original-plus-reduced sale pricing with single-price cards.

Live Site

  • The homepage and search destinations do not continue the specific print, product, or best-seller story promised before the visit.
  • The craftsmanship story is separated from the first three homepage folds and purchase controls.

Competitor Analysis

  • Poupette sits at the high end of the reviewed set, at $330 to $450 in the captured collection.
  • Farm Rio was observed at about $175 to $298, while Sundress Paris was observed at about $85 to $265.
  • The higher price point increases the importance of making the hand-made story visible where shoppers buy.
