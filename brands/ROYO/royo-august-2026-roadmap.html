Conversion testing roadmap

August
2026

Royo has a product customers genuinely value, but slow first impressions, conflicting offers, and uneven proof weaken the path to purchase. This roadmap makes the experience faster, clearer, and easier to trust from first click through cart.

Eight focused ideas

What we want to test

Slot 1A/B Test

Homepage and Landing Page Speed

Homepage and paid-traffic landing page

The homepage and paid-traffic landing page take 11 to 12 seconds to display their main content. That wait interrupts the experience at the moment shoppers expect the promise they clicked to see. The variation keeps the same page but makes the hero visible in under three seconds.

The same storefront appears before intent cools. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 2A/B Test

Site-wide Offer Messaging

Homepage, product pages, and cart

The paid landing page promises 26% off with code NY26, the homepage says up to $15 off, and the product page leads with 15% subscription savings. These competing frames make the value harder to follow across pages. The variation carries the 26% offer through the journey while keeping subscription benefits as an additional value layer.

One offer stays recognizable across the journey, with subscription benefits clearly additive. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 3A/B Test

Build-Your-Own Starter Pack

Royo Starter Pack product page

The Starter Pack fixes the mix at Plain, Cinnamon, Everything, and Artisan Bread, while reviews explicitly ask to choose the four items. A fixed assortment can turn one disliked flavor into a reason not to reorder. The variation lets shoppers build any four-item pack at the same $45 price.

Shoppers replace the fixed mix with four products they choose themselves. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 4A/B Test

Toasting Benefit Message

Product pages and paid-traffic landing page

Dryness appears in more than 15 collected reviews, especially for cinnamon and plain bagels, while customers who toast or add spreads report strong satisfaction. Current product and landing-page copy does not set that texture expectation. The variation adds one practical line: “Best toasted, with your favorite topping.”

A practical serving cue sets the texture expectation before purchase. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 5A/B Test

Homepage Hero Reviews

Homepage

The homepage hero mentions 250,000 happy customers but shows no rating or review count, while the landing page and product page surface that proof prominently. The homepage therefore gives its highest-visibility space a weaker trust stack. The variation adds “18,000+ verified reviews, 4.8-star average” beside the existing customer count.

The existing customer count gains rating quality and verified review volume. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 6A/B Test

Subscription Switch in Cart

Cart drawer

The product page presents subscription as the best value, but a one-time item in the cart has no way to switch without returning to the product page. The cart drops the value story at the last decision point. The variation adds a switch that saves $6.75 now and 15% on every delivery, with the same skip, pause, and cancellation benefits.

A one-time cart item can become a subscription without sending the shopper backward. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 7A/B Test

Homepage Shipping Promise

Homepage

The product page says “Ships Monday-Friday,” but the homepage has no shipping message and the cart leaves the cost as “calculated at checkout.” Reviews also describe an $8 freight charge as an unexpected cost. The variation brings the existing shipping-timing promise directly beneath the homepage call to action.

The homepage CTA gains the same shipping-timing reassurance already used on the product page. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 8A/B Test

Founder Credentials

Paid-traffic landing page

The landing page says “Made By A Nutritionist, Crafted By A Baker” without names or a photo, even though the page later identifies Ronit as a registered dietitian and Yoel as a lifelong baker. The headline makes a credibility claim before it supplies the people behind it. The variation brings their names, roles, and a real founder image into that proof section.

The expertise claim becomes specific through real names, roles, and brand-owned imagery. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Cross-source picture

Biggest Killers of Conversion Rate

  • The product promise is strong, but dryness and flavor limitations create a gap between “tastes like real bread” and some customers’ first bite.
  • Price is the second most common review frustration, while discount framing changes across the paid landing page, homepage, product page, and search.
  • The homepage and paid landing page both have critical LCP failures, so slow rendering affects the journey before the product story can work.
  • Review volume, guarantees, and shipping promises already exist, but they appear unevenly across the homepage, product page, landing page, and cart.

What each source revealed

Meta Ads and Landing Pages

  • Paid-social creative and its landing page align around low-carb, high-protein baked goods, strong taste language, and a 26% discount code.
  • The landing page shows 21,000+ verified reviews, a 5-star rating, a money-back guarantee, and clear calorie and net-carb comparisons.
  • The homepage switches to “Up to $15 OFF,” so shoppers encounter a different offer outside the paid landing experience.

Google Ads

  • Search creative leads with low carb, keto-friendly, and high protein, while the paid-social journey leads with emotion and discount.
  • No specific discount is visible in the search-ad screenshots.
  • Search references 10,786 reviews, while the paid landing page displays 21,000+ verified reviews.

Reviews and UGC

  • Taste, satiety, guilt-free indulgence, health outcomes, and convenience are recurring strengths.
  • Dryness appears in 15+ reviews, with cinnamon and plain bagels mentioned most often. Toasting and spreads are common workarounds.
  • Price is mentioned negatively in 20+ reviews, and shoppers compare Royo’s $2-or-more bagels with regular bread at $1.25 per bagel.
  • Customers explicitly ask for control over the Starter Pack mix, while 5+ reviews also mention freshness, short counts, or changing product size.

PageSpeed

  • Homepage LCP is 12,709 ms and paid landing-page LCP is 11,416 ms, versus the 2,500 ms threshold.
  • Homepage JavaScript bootup takes 2,974 ms. TTFB is 11 ms, which points to rendering rather than server response as the bottleneck.
  • Both pages have good layout stability, with CLS at 0 on the homepage and 0.011 on the paid landing page.

Site Screenshots

  • The homepage hero shows 250,000+ happy customers but no rating or review count.
  • The Starter Pack product page shows 18,295 reviews, a 15% subscription option, a 30-day guarantee, and “Ships Monday-Friday” near the add-to-cart area.
  • The cart drawer shows spend-threshold savings and product upsells, but no guarantee, delivery estimate, or subscription switch for one-time items.

Live Homepage Analysis

  • The homepage repeats “Buy More, Save More. Up to $15 off” in the announcement bar and hero.
  • The first fold pairs a red Shop Now button with the promise “Get the same great taste you love or your money back.”
  • Shipping timing is not stated in the homepage folds reviewed.
