CRO testing roadmap

September 2026

Bootylicious has a strong product promise and a standout Build Your Box experience, but key nutrition proof, flavor guidance, and purchase reassurance arrive too late. This roadmap makes the fastest wins visible first, then sharpens the path from paid promise to completed box.

Eight focused moves

Each idea turns a documented friction point into a clearer customer experience.

Slot 1Immediate Fix

Mobile Load Speed

Page: Homepage and Build Your Box PDP

The audit measured a 7.3-second homepage LCP and a 6.8-second PDP LCP on mobile, with roughly 800 KiB of unused JavaScript on each page. Fast server response means the delay sits in the front end. Strip nonessential work from the first render so the product promise and primary action appear sooner.

The optimized first render prioritizes the product promise and primary action. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 2A/B Test

Cart Upsell Replacement

Page: Cart drawer

The current upgrade feed still puts the $5 1/8 Measuring Cup first even though it is unavailable, while the $4 Bootylicious Recipe Booklet is available. The first upgrade slot leads to a dead end. Lead with the live booklet and remove the scoop until it is buyable.

The available Bootylicious Recipe Booklet leads the cart upgrades, while the scoop stays out until it is buyable. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 3A/B Test

Nutrition on PDP Load

Page: Single-flavor product pages

Double Chocolate already shows 25g protein per cup in the buy-box fold, but Nutrition Highlights remains closed by default. The 7g net carbs and 14-18g dietary fiber claims still require another action to find. Open the nutrition section on load while keeping the visible protein promise and the other product sections unchanged.

The existing 25g protein line stays in the buy box, while carbs and fiber become visible without another tap. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 4A/B Test

Homepage Purchase Path

Page: Homepage and product journey

The hero points shoppers to Build Your Box, but the next product carousel gives single-flavor cards equal weight and a faster Add to Cart path. The page promotes one journey, then undercuts it. Lead the carousel with a larger Build Your Box card and keep single flavors visible as secondary choices.

Build Your Box becomes the unmistakable first step while single-flavor shopping remains available. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 5A/B Test

Paid-Promise Hero Match

Page: Homepage

Offer-focused paid traffic promises a daily cheat-day feeling and a restock, but the shared homepage does not repeat either message. That specific hook disappears at the click. Show a tailored version of the existing hero that continues the restock and cheat-day promise without changing its layout or CTA.

The landing hero repeats the promise that brought this traffic to the site. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 6A/B Test

Flavor-First Box Builder

Page: Build Your Box

The builder asks shoppers to choose a 12, 24, or 36 pack before they see the flavor grid. That puts price commitment ahead of product discovery. Let shoppers choose flavors first, then present the matching case size and price from the number selected.

Flavor discovery comes before case-size commitment, with the progress bar preserving momentum. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 7A/B Test

Reassurance at the CTA

Page: Homepage and product detail pages

The audit found no shipping-time or policy line beside the homepage and PDP purchase actions, while the current cart code now carries a money-back banner. Reassurance is present, but inconsistent across the journey. Add one concise, policy-backed line beneath the homepage and PDP CTAs and keep its promise aligned with the cart.

A single policy-backed promise follows the shopper from product discovery through checkout. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Slot 8A/B Test

Flavor Recommendation Prompt

Page: Build Your Box

The Variety Pack rates 3.7/5 on Amazon versus 4.2/5 for single-flavor products, and on-site reviews also describe first-order flavor mismatch. Build Your Box names Double Chocolate, Birthday Cake, and Cinnamon Bun in a light Fan faves line, but it does not turn those suggestions into a selectable starting point. Add a Customer Favorites row with those flavors plus Red Velvet before the full grid.

The existing Fan faves hint becomes a selectable Customer Favorites starter row. Concept illustration only. Final design will differ.

Biggest Killers of Conversion Rate

  • Dead cart momentum

    Subscription is emphasized earlier in the funnel, but the cart leads its upgrade carousel with an unavailable measuring cup.

  • Broken message continuity

    Core protein claims match well, but restock, cheat-day, fiber, payment, and shipping promises do not carry consistently into the shared homepage.

  • Flavor discovery risk

    On-site reviews and Amazon ratings both point to specific flavor mismatch, especially when shoppers sample a mixed selection.

What the Data Shows

Meta Ads and Landing Pages

  • Protein, calorie, and zero-regret messaging carries cleanly from the dominant paid-social creative into the homepage hero.
  • Restock and cheat-day messaging disappears after the click, even though that traffic lands on the same homepage.
  • The FIRST15 offer appears in the collected creative and the current homepage announcement content.

Google Ads

  • Search and shopping creative reinforces 25g protein and the product's macro positioning.
  • Fiber, payment-option, and free-shipping claims appear in Google creative but not in the collected homepage folds.
  • Sitelinks route to Create Your Own Box and Variety Pack, which aligns with the site's real shopping paths.

Reviews and UGC

  • Shoppers repeatedly praise Build Your Box because it lets them avoid a pre-selected assortment.
  • Double Chocolate, Birthday Cake, Red Velvet, and Cinnamon Bun cluster as repeat favorites.
  • Carrot Cake, Lemon Poppy Seed, and Blueberry receive repeated criticism for flavor or texture inconsistency.
  • One first-order shopper needed support to recalibrate after choosing flavors they disliked.

Page Speed Insights

  • Homepage and PDP both scored 54/100 on mobile.
  • Largest Contentful Paint measured 7.3s on the homepage and 6.8s on the PDP.
  • Server response was 10ms on both pages, while unused JavaScript reached 794 KiB and 816 KiB.
  • The PDP recorded 17.9s of main-thread work. Layout shift was not a material issue.

Competitor Analysis

  • Bootylicious carries the highest full-price per-cup cost among the reviewed brands, paired with the highest protein claim.
  • Kodiak Cakes and Premier Protein do not match the Build Your Box flavor-customization mechanic.
  • The flavor picker is the clearest structural differentiator to protect and explain.

Social and Community Research

  • The 25g protein, low-net-carb, 60-second-prep story is consistent across creator content and brand messaging.
  • Independent creators repeatedly describe the pre-microwave texture as wet or gooey, but the collected on-site reviews did not confirm that concern.
  • The Amazon Variety Pack holds a 3.7/5 rating across 1,120 ratings, while reviewed single-flavor products sit at 4.2/5.

Site Screenshots

  • The homepage hero leads to Build Your Box, while the next carousel also offers direct single-flavor Add to Cart actions.
  • The box builder places case-size selection before flavor selection and preselects Subscribe and Save.
  • Nutrition Highlights remains closed by default. Double Chocolate shows 25g protein in the buy box, while 7g net carbs and 14-18g fiber remain behind the disclosure.
  • The cart drawer led its upgrade carousel with a $5 measuring cup marked out of stock.
